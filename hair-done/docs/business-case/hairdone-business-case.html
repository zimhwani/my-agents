What this doesn't know

  • Every "guess" is a starting point. The first three months of real bookings will replace most of them.
  • The roll-out copies Airbnb's: line up pros in a city before clients arrive, open the next city once the playbook works, and charge both sides (Hair Done's $3 from the client plus 12% from the pro is the same split). Airbnb also spent heavily on marketing as it grew. With paid marketing off, every city here grows by word of mouth alone.
  • New pros only join while the ones there are busy enough, but pros already signed up don't leave any faster when it's quiet. Real pros drift off if Hair Done sends them too little work.
  • Bookings a pro wants but no client books, and clients who find no one free, are simply lost. Real people wait, or leave for good.
  • Populations are each country's latest official metro estimates (mostly mid-2025). A city's price level is the median Fresha listing for a gel manicure and a women's cut on 30 Oct 2026, in Australian dollars: small samples of 10 to 66 salons, before tips. That's why Auckland, Toronto and Vancouver come out cheaper than Melbourne and Canberra dearer. Overseas sales taxes and VAT, currency swings and local licences aren't in it.
  • Stripe prices are Stripe's own published ones for each country, read on 30 Oct 2026, with fixed fees turned into Australian dollars at that day's rates. Australia's Connect page still says 1.7% where the pricing page says 1.65%. Payout fees are counted once per booking, which overstates them.
  • Market size: IBISWorld puts Australian hair and beauty services at $12.5bn in 2026 (about $450 a person); the ABS's household spending weights point lower, closer to $8bn. No one publishes how much of it goes to mobile pros. The closest at-home business, Glamsquad in the US, is about US$20M a year in an US$85bn salon market. Priv, a US beauty app, shrank because travel time ate into what pros earned.
  • GST: once Hair Done's Australian fees pass $75,000 in 12 months, the model registers it and sends 1/11 of those fees to the ATO from the next month. Whether the full booking value counts instead is a question for your accountant.
  • No income tax, no salaries unless you add them to running costs, no salary for you unless you set an hourly rate, no investment or loans.
