# Terms: putting liability for the work on the pro (draft for legal review, not live)

Keith asked (29 Oct 2026) for wording that puts liability for anything that goes wrong with a pro's
service on the pro, not on Hair Done. This is a plain-English draft to take to a lawyer, not legal advice.

## What the live terms already say (site/terms/, 25 Oct 2026)

- **§2:** Hair Done is a marketplace. Pros run their own businesses; the client's agreement for the
  service is with the pro. Hair Done doesn't provide beauty services.
- **§9:** pros are independent, and responsible for their own tax, licences, insurance and products.
- **§10:** Hair Done checks pros but doesn't supervise their work or guarantee outcomes.
- **§11:** to the extent the law allows, Hair Done isn't liable for the services a pro provides; no
  indirect loss; total liability capped at 12 months of fees; ACL consumer guarantees preserved.

## What's missing

1. **No indemnity from the pro.** §11 says Hair Done isn't liable, but nothing says the pro covers Hair
   Done if a client claims against it anyway. A client who is burnt or has a reaction can still sue
   Hair Done; an indemnity is what lets Hair Done recover that from the pro.
2. **Insurance isn't required.** §9 says insurance is the pro's responsibility, not that they must have
   it. An indemnity from a pro with no insurance and no money is worth very little. The vetting page has
   an "I've seen their insurance" tick, but nothing asks pros for it.
3. **The pro agreement's liability section is a placeholder** (`pro-agreement-draft.md` §11).

## What the law won't let the terms do

- **Consumer guarantees can't be signed away.** Under the Australian Consumer Law, services come with
  guarantees (due care and skill, fit for purpose). Terms can't exclude them, and saying so can itself be
  misleading. The live terms already respect this.
- **Unfair contract terms.** Since 9 November 2023, unfair terms in standard-form contracts are illegal
  and carry penalties, for contracts with consumers and with small businesses. Pros are small
  businesses. A one-sided term that makes a pro cover Hair Done even for Hair Done's own mistakes is the
  kind of term that can be found unfair, which voids it and exposes Hair Done to penalties. So the
  indemnity below covers what the pro causes, and expressly not what Hair Done causes.
- **Contractor, not employee.** Heavy control over how pros work, plus heavy liability on them, can weigh
  toward them being workers rather than businesses, including under the 2024 "employee-like" rules for
  platform workers. Worth the lawyer's eye alongside the pro agreement's §2 note.

## Proposed wording

### Terms §9 For pros: new "Responsibility for your work"

> You're responsible for the services you provide and how you provide them, including the products and
> tools you use. You agree to cover Hair Done for any claim, loss or cost (including reasonable legal
> costs) that comes from: the services you provide; anything you do or don't do at a booking, including
> injury to anyone or damage to property; or you breaking these terms or the law. This doesn't apply to
> the extent the claim, loss or cost was caused by Hair Done.

### Terms §9 For pros: new "Insurance"

> You must hold public liability insurance of at least $10 million [lawyer and insurer to confirm the
> amount, and whether treatment or professional indemnity cover is also needed for lashes, brows and
> chemical services], keep it current, and send us a copy of your certificate when you join and each
> time it renews. We may stop showing you to clients if it lapses.

### Terms §11 Liability: one added line

> If a client makes a claim against Hair Done about a pro's service, we may ask the pro to deal with it
> directly, and the pro's responsibility under section 9 applies.

### Pro agreement §11

The same two clauses as Terms §9, plus: how Hair Done tells a pro about a claim, that the pro helps
defend it, and that Hair Done can hold a payout while a claim about that booking is open (only if the
lawyer agrees that's fair).

## Also worth doing, outside the terms

- **Hair Done's own insurance.** Marketplace or platform liability cover for Hair Done itself. The
  indemnity only helps if the pro can pay.
- **Ask for the certificate in vetting.** Once insurance is required, the ID check should ask for the
  certificate and the vetting email should flag a missing or expired one. (The app has no upload for it
  yet; until then pros could email it to hello@.)
- **Tell people before it takes effect.** The terms (§12) promise notice before a change that matters.
  This is one: email current pros and clients, then publish.
