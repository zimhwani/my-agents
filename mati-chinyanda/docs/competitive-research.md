# Competitive Research — Premium Speaker / MC / Host / Fashion-Founder Sites

**Client:** Mati Chinyanda — speaker, MC & event host, co-founder/co-host of the podcast *What Left The Group Chat*, co-founder of *FreekÀ Runway* (Melbourne multicultural runway platform), formerly FashionOne.
**Primary conversion:** book Mati as speaker / MC / host. **Secondary:** engage her for event production & creative direction.
**Prepared by:** Design strategy team (Brand Guardian + UI Designer + UX Architect) — 2026-09-22

## Research notes and caveats

- Client verification (web search): *What Left The Group Chat* is an Australian podcast (launched 2025) hosted by three Black women — one Zimbabwean, one African American, one Zambian — covering life in Australia, business/career/ambition, marriage/friendship/womanhood, culture and identity. Listed on Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music, Spotify and the Great Australian Pods directory. *FreekÀ Runway* is described as "a multi-culturally inspired runway show in celebration of how far ethnic communities have come within the creative space in Melbourne … a platform for emerging creatives to express themselves through fashion, dance, art and music", with Mati Chinyanda as co-founder (source: freekarunway.com "EXPRESS : YOUR : SELF"). Melbourne is confirmed; a Monash University program-coordinator background surfaced in search — treat as unverified and do not publish without client sign-off.
- Direct page fetches were blocked by the network egress proxy for every site below, so pattern notes combine search-engine summaries, published case studies (e.g. OFF+BRAND's Trevor Noah case study, Awwwards SOTD listing) and the team's working knowledge of these sites. Anything marked **(verify)** should be eyeballed in a browser before it is quoted to the client.

## The eight benchmarks

### 1. Trevor Noah — trevornoah.com (OFF+BRAND, Awwwards SOTD)
- **What it is:** an "immersive digital home" uniting stand-up, podcast, books and world tour. Two-colour system (#FF9BB4 pink on #1D2440 navy) — one bold accent, one deep base.
- **Premium signals:** WebGL/scroll-driven storytelling, oversized display type, personality-led copy, live tour data. Content hub architecture (shows → books → podcast) from one nav.
- **Lesson / caveat:** Awwwards reviewers praised aesthetics (7.45/10) but flagged that the layout was not mobile-friendly. Motion must never cost mobile usability — for Mati, planners will open the site on a phone between meetings.
- **Adopt:** a tight two-tone palette with one unmistakable accent; a "hub" nav where every venture is one click away; a personality-first hero.
- **Avoid:** WebGL and heavy scroll hijacking; anything that breaks on a 375px viewport.

### 2. Bozoma Saint John — bozomasaintjohn.com ("Let's Live Urgently… TOGETHER")
- **What it is:** a personal-brand hub for an executive/keynote speaker/author. Hero leads with a tagline and a credential line ("#1 most influential CMO — Forbes"). Sub-brands (The Urgent Life book, The Badass Workshop) get their own destinations.
- **Premium signals:** bold, editorial photography of a Black woman in colour and joy; short manifesto-style copy; a strong personal descriptor ("Badass Boz") that functions as IP; speaking handled through bureaus (Harry Walker, Chartwell) with the personal site as the authority layer.
- **Adopt:** an ownable phrase above the fold; a credential line under the name; treating sub-ventures (podcast, FreekÀ) as branded satellites of the person, not competitors for the hero.
- **Avoid:** sending booking traffic off-site to a bureau by default — Mati books direct, so the form must live on her domain.

### 3. Elaine Welteroth — elainewelteroth.com
- **What it is:** journalist / TV host / NYT-bestselling author; youngest EIC in Condé Nast history. Bureau pages quote her fee range ($32–60k) and lead with three-line bios.
- **Premium signals (verify):** editorial serif display type, magazine-style full-bleed portraits, "as seen in" press strip, book/TV/speaking split into clear sections, muted palette letting photography carry colour.
- **Adopt:** magazine crops and generous white space; press-logo strip early; the three-length bio pattern (one line / one paragraph / full) which planners literally copy into run-sheets.
- **Avoid:** burying the speaking CTA under book promotion.

### 4. Ashley Graham — ashleygraham.com
- **What it is:** model / host / entrepreneur; fashion-forward personal site.
- **Premium signals (verify):** high-fashion photography, monochrome UI so the images pop, large uppercase sans nav, Instagram feed integration, product/partnership tiles.
- **Adopt:** monochrome UI + colour only in photography; a fashion-forward gallery grid (directly relevant to FreekÀ Runway).
- **Avoid:** e-commerce style tiles for a speaker site — they dilute the single booking goal.

### 5. Simon Sinek — simonsinek.com/speaking
- **What it is:** the canonical high-converting speaking page. Roundups describe "funky, loud fonts", orange/purple accents, generous white space and muted greys against vibrant photos.
- **Premium signals:** showreel high on the page; topic cards with a one-line promise each; testimonial pull-quotes with names and logos; a single, repeated CTA ("Book Simon to speak" / "Inquire"); FAQ-style logistics for planners.
- **Adopt:** the speaking-page skeleton — reel → topics → proof → logistics → form; a two-CTA rule (book + watch).
- **Avoid:** seven competing CTAs and nav items.

### 6. Brené Brown — brenebrown.com
- **What it is:** speaker/author/podcaster hub with a warm, calm, serif-led identity.
- **Premium signals (verify):** warm neutral palette, serif headlines with humanist sans body, podcast section with latest episodes and platform buttons, "Dare to Lead" hub, restrained motion.
- **Adopt:** the podcast block pattern (cover art, latest 3 episodes, listen-on buttons); warm neutrals rather than cold black/white.
- **Avoid:** over-long pages that mix retail, courses and speaking — Mati's site should stay focused.

### 7. Ben Sorensen — bensorensen1.com/host (Melbourne MC, local benchmark)
- **What it is:** the direct local competitor pattern: Melbourne corporate MC/host with 20 years' experience, client wall (Australian Open, AFL, IBM, Infosys, RACV, Coldplay), named testimonials ("A wit so sharp you might want to wear Kevlar — Chris Do").
- **Premium signals:** named-client logo wall, showreel, testimonials attributed to real people/companies, listing on ICMI/Crowdpleaser/EntertainOz bureaus for reach.
- **Lesson:** local MC sites are proof-heavy but visually generic (template feel). This is Mati's opening — same rigour of proof, far higher design ceiling.
- **Adopt:** named testimonials with role + organisation; a client/venue wall; bureau listings as a secondary distribution channel.
- **Avoid:** stock-template layouts, small centred text blocks, generic blue CTAs.

### 8. Awwwards-tier editorial portfolios (composite: Muzli "Top 100 Portfolio Websites 2025", Awwwards Portfolio winners, e.g. Studio Null, We Are Example, Grit Pictures)
- **What they are:** typography-led, image-forward portfolios — "bold textures, torn edges, collage details, monochrome styling" (Grit Pictures), oversized serif display, editorial grid rhythm, scroll-triggered reveals, marquees, cursor-aware hover.
- **Premium signals:** one dominant typeface at huge sizes; asymmetric grids with big gutters; grain/duotone treatment unifying disparate photos; sticky section labels; slow, eased motion; a footer that is itself a CTA.
- **Adopt:** editorial grid, oversized serif, image grain/tint to unify, marquee for "as seen at", reveal-on-scroll with `IntersectionObserver`, a big-type footer CTA.
- **Avoid:** custom cursors, preloaders, horizontal-scroll sections, text set below 16px for "elegance".

## Cross-cutting findings (from the 2026 speaker-site roundups)

Roundups by SpeakrBrand, Talks.co, Speaker Advisor (AU), NanoGlobals and MW Designs converge on the same conversion traits:

1. **One ownable phrase above the fold** — a trademark-able idea that is the speaker's IP.
2. **Two CTAs, not seven** — one booking action paired with one proof action (watch reel / download kit).
3. **Visual social proof inside the hero** — logo bar, testimonial, clip or ranking signal in the first viewport.
4. **Prove credibility in the first scroll** — named clients, demo reel, testimonial.
5. **Booking path visible on every speaking-related page** — sticky "Book" in nav plus a form.
6. **Qualifying inquiry form** — event date, location (in-person/virtual), audience size, budget range (optional), plus automated speaker-kit delivery on submit.
7. **Downloadable speaker kit / one-sheet** placed near the form (bio lengths, headshots, AV rider, topic summaries).
8. **Mobile speed is non-negotiable** — planners browse on phones between meetings.

## Patterns to avoid

- WebGL, scroll hijacking, horizontal-scroll sections, preloaders, custom cursors — Trevor Noah's mobile criticism is the warning.
- Sending bookings to a bureau by default; the form lives on matichinyanda.com (bureau listings are supplementary).
- Retail/course/product tiles competing with the booking goal.
- More than 6 nav items; more than two CTA styles per view.
- Grey-on-grey "luxury" text below 4.5:1; type under 16px for body.
- Generic template layouts, stock imagery, centred narrow text on every section.
- Autoplaying sound; autoplaying video without a poster and pause control.
- Unattributed testimonials ("— Client").
